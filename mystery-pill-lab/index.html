        How to play

        The job

        • Grab a pill from the tray (button, G, or click one).
        • Read both faces. Debossed imprints are nearly invisible under flat light: drop the lamp angle to rake light across them, sweep the direction, switch the pad color for white pills, and use the loupe.
        • Search the reference by imprint, color and shape. Check size and mass with calipers and the balance; counterfeits measure wrong.
        • Give the pill to the SIM-HUMAN or discard it. You can only give a pill you have positively identified.

        Keeping them alive

        • Read the chart first: allergies, home meds already on board, kidneys, liver, alcohol.
        • Drugs follow real absorption and half-lives. Peaks come later, methadone and diazepam linger for days, and acetaminophen liver injury shows up 1-3 days after the dose.
        • Watch the monitor, the exam (pupils, skin, clonus) and the PK screen. Order labs; results arrive after a realistic delay.
        • The crash cart has oxygen, airway, BVM, naloxone, NAC, bicarbonate, cyproheptadine, epinephrine, CPR and a defibrillator.

        Controls

        • Drag to orbit, right-drag or Shift-drag to pan, wheel or pinch to zoom.
        • On the loupe stage, dragging turns the pill.
        • Space pause, G grab, F flip, C crash cart.
        • X-ray mode labels the organs. Speed up time to watch drugs wear off; alarms slow it back down.

        Read this

        This is a cartoon simulation for learning. Pill appearances are modeled on public pill-identifier references but may be simplified, and the pharmacology is approximate. Never identify or take real pills with a game. For a real unknown pill, ask a pharmacist. For a possible poisoning, call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 (US) or 911.

        Mystery Pill Lab

        A huge evidence bag of unidentified pills, from OTC painkillers to Schedule II narcotics, plus a few counterfeits. Identify each one by imprint, shape, color and size, then decide: give it to the SIM-HUMAN or bin it. The SIM-HUMAN has working organs, blood flow and drug metabolism, and reacts like a real patient.

        1. Grab a pill and inspect it under the lamp.
        2. Search the reference and confirm a positive ID.
        3. Think about allergies, interactions and timing, then give or discard.
        4. Treat the patient's complaints and keep them alive until the end of the shift.

        Educational cartoon simulation, not medical advice. Never identify or take real pills based on a game. Poison Control (US): 1-800-222-1222.
